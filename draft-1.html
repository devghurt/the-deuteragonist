The Deuteragonist

Deuteragonist
noun - the character next in importance to the protagonist, esp the antagonist

PROLOGUE

"Who are you, really?"
The wind gave no reply. As expected.
It had been hours since his last contact with this enigmatic voice that would whisper lies, propaganda and incredulous hypotheticals into his 'measly mortal brain'. Everytime, he would bang his head onto his writing desk to make it stop stop stop. It proved effective. For a while. Then it would start again, a never-ending cycle of teasing, mockery and pestering.
He swallowed a gulp, his throat dry and scratchy.
"I don't expect you to give me any answer, but the least you could do is save these poor souls from what's coming next."
Radio silence. As expected.
"Fine." The guy clenched his perpetually ink-stained hands. He spread his arms wide, posing as if he were a sacrificial offering splayed onto a cloth. "If you want me to be your villain, I will be," he yelled to no one in particular, though he despairingly hoped it would reach someone.
I'm sick and tired of this life anyway. It hurt to be the only one in the room, no -- the only one in the world who understood the inner workings of his universe, like some self-aware character in a TV show who occasionally breaks the fourth wall for the sake of comedy.
"I don't know what you're talking about," he mumbles in a strained voice. Then in a barely audible tone, he adds, "I wish I'd never touched a pen and paper in my life."
Go, a silky voice in his head coaxes, ruin their lives.
It's all fiction anyway, would it even hurt? Truthfully, the only one who would be hurting is me.
This time, he doesn't disobey.

CHAPTER 1

I think I would spontaneously combust if I were to tell anyone of my problems.
"Are you okay? You look out of it." "Yeah, I'm fine. Was just nervous for the test today. Couldn't sleep well."
It technically wouldn't be considered lying if it were just the half-truth, right? I mean, I frankly had been stressing myself out for this test the whole week.
"Your eyes are red and puffy, were you--?" "Oh, I was just really sleepy and kept rubbing my eyes. Sorry."
It's just it wasn't the full picture. No one would blame me for keeping some things to myself, right?
Right?
Someone was snapping their fingers in quick succession, one of the few things that got on my nerves.
A suspiciously human-shaped blur made its way into my peripheral. "Earth to Drew, earth to Drew-"
"Go away," I chided, pushing her face out of sight.
"Stop daydreaming, we're going to get our results soon."
Right, the results. My stomach started flitting restlessly, moths due breaking out of their cocoons.
My best friend, or better known as June, plopped onto her seat in a contented way, probably triumphing at her success at pissing me off.
The bell rang, drowning out the chatter and whatever negativity was starting to seep into my thoughts. The door opened and gave way, but the person who came through was not our teacher. In fact, he looked as if he were just a freshly graduated college student.
"Did Mr. Lee travel back in time?" June joked beside me.
I forced myself to display a small smile, her quips always helped unknot the ever-growing tangle inside my stomach, yet I couldn't help but sense something off.
"Hi," the stranger started, supposedly meant to sound like an amiable greeting but came out a cracked mess. He cleared his throat, loosened the tie restricting the blood flow around his neck, and started again, "Um... I'm Mr. Keating. Your teacher, uh..." Then in a not-so-soft voice mumbled, "What was his name again? Uhh..."
"Mr. Lee," June offered.
"Yes!" he snapped his fingers in revelation. "That guy." I had to refrain from snorting. "He kind of... Well, better to be honest about it." He slammed the books he was carrying with one strident thump, and continued, "He's disappeared."
The entirety of the classroom erupted in discombobulated remarks. Throw in some expletives there. My feelings towards Mr. Lee were neutral, but I became instantly enraptured by this news.
Mr. Keating cleared his throat again, cutting through the noise. He went on, "From now on, I'll be your English teacher until this whole... mess resolves."
Some particularly exhilarated cheers resonated throughout the room.
"Were you supposed to tell us this?" one student piped up.
"Ah, shoot... The principal told me not to, but I forgot. Well, you guys should be fine not spreading this around, right?"
The sound of snickering absolutely negated that thought. Nevertheless, he seemed to shrug it off.
"Where did the class leave off? Ah, yes, the results," he began to tinker with the vintage computer from the early 2000s, the ones the school had never bothered to replace since it came from a time when tech was durable.
"Director of the light and sound team," he squinted his eyes for a better look, "Emmie Samson." Thunderous claps for this Emmie person emanated from a small group packed together tightly, presumably her friend group.
"Director of the props team, Jon Greenway." More applause from keen ears.
"And lead of the scriptwriters-- Drew Collins." My heart sank deeper in my chest, red strings bound so taut I forgot how to breathe. Disappointment never failed me, it seems. The classroom filled with impatient applause, a cacophony of out-of-sync strikes of flesh upon flesh, just like--
No, don't think about it, I thought, resisting the urge to smack my head on the wooden desk. Don't you dare, don't you dare, don't you dare--
"Now the role you've all been waiting for--" Mr. Keating smiled brightly "-- June Endez! As the director of our play."
Of course, of course, of course. Sucking in a deep breath, I thought of how hard I would have to clench my fists before my overgrown fingernails could puncture skin and draw blood.
"You got the role," I told her, faux joy plastered on my face. I tried to ignore how my cheeks hurt after smiling this long.
But she was too preoccupied to notice me. Her expression was one of disbelief and wonderment.
"Wait-- wait, teacher, but I want to be the main actress."
He stopped perusing the list which had been given to him to look up at her. "Yeah, you can be, once we hold the auditions."
"But what about my being the director?"
"Being both is not mutually exclusive."
"Yes, what about--" I started, but my voice felt like sandpaper, beginning to drift off as I explained, "-- Mr. Lee said we couldn't be two leading roles at once."
Mr. Keating contemplated this for a while, complete with the face scrunched in thought and chin resting on palm, he obviously had a flair for the dramatics. "Well, I say," he said, cadence rising in crescendo, "I can allow both."
It's fine, it's alright, I tried to reassure myself, there's nothing wrong with being a scriptwriter. Nothing...
"So, this year's theme is Legend with a Twist. Pick whatever urban legend, myth, anything that's at least of interest. I don't have anything else to talk about," he said. "That's it. Class is dismissed."

CHAPTER 2

The day wasn't even halfway over, yet word had already spread across the campus like wildfire. "Did you hear about Mr. Lee? I heard he--" "Do you think he went missing? I think--" "-- seems a bit suspicious--" Normally, I would want to stuff cotton inside my ears to drown out the noise, but today eavesdropping on conversations that didn't concern me had piqued my interest. All of a sudden, hot breath began to tickle my ear and a voice muttered, "People don't just go missing for no reason." That took me by surprise, I jumped up and almost scrambled out of my seat. "June, what the heck?" "Futher proof you're a scaredy cat," she said, another look of triumph across her face. "No, I--" I stopped, eyeing the packet of chips in her hand. "Are those my chips? Why do you have-- Never mind." Packet crinkling gloriously, she took one magnificently circular chip out, as if to flaunt the result of her thieving. With a crunch, she continued, "I digress, it feels like someone just dragged Mr. Keating into this, and he's going along with it." "Imagine he's the one who kidnapped Mr. Lee," I joked. She gave me a look. "Who the hell would want to teach this cohort?" I shrugged. "A-anyway," I cleared my throat, heart palpitating all of a sudden once I'd recalled what to discuss with her, "are you sure you're going to be director? I mean -- not that I don't think you could do it -- the opposite, actually -- but maybe the pressure would be too much to handle, you get me? Also, what legend would be suitable to take inspiration from? I--" "Of course I'd do it," she intercepted, raising a brow at me as if that question were the most obvious thing in the world. It was, and I felt ashamed for even doubting that for a second.
