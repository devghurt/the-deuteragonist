The Deuteragonist

Deuteragonist
noun - the character next in importance to the protagonist, esp the antagonist

PROLOGUE

"Who are you, really?"
The wind gave no reply. As expected.
It had been hours since his last contact with this enigmatic voice that would whisper lies, propaganda and incredulous hypotheticals into his 'measly mortal brain'. Everytime, he would bang his head onto his writing desk to make it stop stop stop. It proved effective. For a while. Then it would start again, a never-ending cycle of teasing, mockery and pestering.
He swallowed a gulp, his throat dry and scratchy.
"I don't expect you to give me any answer, but the least you could do is save these poor souls from what's coming next."
Radio silence. As expected.
"Fine." The guy clenched his perpetually ink-stained hands. He spread his arms wide, posing as if he were a sacrificial offering splayed onto a cloth. "If you want me to be your villain, I will be," he yelled to no one in particular, though he despairingly hoped it would reach someone.
I'm sick and tired of this life anyway. It hurt to be the only one in the room, no -- the only one in the world who understood the inner workings of his universe, like some self-aware character in a TV show who occasionally breaks the fourth wall for the sake of comedy.
"I don't know what you're talking about," he mumbles in a strained voice. Then in a barely audible tone, he adds, "I wish I'd never touched a pen and paper in my life."
Go, a silky voice in his head coaxes, *ruin their lives.*
It's all fiction anyway, would it even hurt? Truthfully, the only one who would be hurting is me.
This time, he doesn't disobey.

CHAPTER 1

I think I would spontaneously combust if I were to tell anyone of my problems.
"Are you okay? You look out of it." "Yeah, I'm fine. Was just nervous for the test today. Couldn't sleep well."
It technically wouldn't be considered lying if it were just the half-truth, right? I mean, I frankly had been stressing myself out for this test the whole week.
"Your eyes are red and puffy, were you--?" "Oh, I was just really sleepy and kept rubbing my eyes. Sorry."
It's just it wasn't the full picture. No one would blame me for keeping some things to myself, right?
Right?
Someone was snapping their fingers in quick succession, one of the few things that got on my nerves.
A suspiciously human-shaped blur made its way into my peripheral. "Earth to Drew, earth to Drew-"
"Go away," I chided, pushing her face out of sight.
"Stop daydreaming, we're going to get our results soon."
Right, the results. My stomach started flitting restlessly, moths due breaking out of their cocoons.
My best friend, or better known as June, plopped onto her seat in a contented way, probably triumphing at her success at pissing me off.
The bell rang, drowning out the chatter and whatever negativity was starting to seep into my thoughts. The door opened and gave way, but the person who came through was not our teacher. In fact, he looked as if he were just a freshly graduated college student.
"Did Mr. Lee travel back in time?" June joked beside me.
I forced myself to display a small smile, her quips always helped unknot the ever-growing tangle inside my stomach, yet I couldn't help but sense something off.
"Hi," the stranger started, supposedly meant to sound like an amiable greeting but came out a cracked mess. He cleared his throat, loosened the tie restricting the blood flow around his neck, and started again, "Um... I'm Mr. Keating. Your teacher, uh..." Then in a not-so-soft voice mumbled, "What was his name again? Uhh..."
"Mr. Lee," June offered.
"Yes!" he snapped his fingers in revelation. "That guy." I had to refrain from snorting. "He kind of... Well, better to be honest about it." He slammed the books he was carrying with one strident thump, and continued, "He's disappeared."
The entirety of the classroom erupted in discombobulated remarks. Throw in some expletives there. "What the heck?" "I'm not dreaming, right?" (said person also stretched their arm out for somebody to pinch them) "He was pretty lame anyway." "Coooool..." My feelings towards Mr. Lee were neutral, but I became instantly enraptured by this news.
Mr. Keating cleared his throat again, cutting through the noise. He went on, "From now on, I'll be your English teacher until this whole... mess resolves."
Some particularly exhilarated cheers resonated throughout the room.
"Were you supposed to tell us this?" oneyou student piped up.
"Ah, shoot... The principal told me not to, but I forgot. Well, you guys should be fine not spreading this around, right?"
The sound of snickering absolutely negated that thought. Nevertheless, he seemed to have shrugged it off.
